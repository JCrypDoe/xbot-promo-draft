More than a game token

Your privacy

Your history belongs to you. You decide who learns what, and a company can confirm a fact about you without ever seeing who you are.

Ads on your terms

Sponsors pay you for your attention instead of interrupting it. You opt in, and you get paid.

A real voice

Every player gets one vote to use, or hand to someone they trust. It runs on Cardano, a community-run network, and Earth3 is working toward linking up with Cardano delegates and their treasury.

See it in action: the fish sandwich

  1. Order. Player Doe orders a fish sandwich in a fast-food promotion. That is a moment in the digital world (Earth2), and it gets stamped as a moMintNTime.
  2. Pick up. Doe picks the sandwich up at the counter. That is a moment in the real world (Earth1), and it gets a second stamp.
  3. Power. Every stamp adds to Doe's PlayerPower.
  4. Privacy. Today, details like this mostly end up with a few giant companies. With Earth3, Doe holds the record. A sponsor can ask "was this a real customer?" and get a yes, without learning who Doe is. It works like showing a "21 and over" stamp instead of your whole ID.

A world of agents

Soon you won't hunt through apps and menus. You'll tell a digital helper, called an agent, what you want, and it will do the work.

As computers and phones put these helpers up front, they will have core access to nearly everything a player needs: accounts, money, files, and games. That is powerful, and it raises a fair question: what is your agent allowed to do, and who decides?

Earth3 organizes it. It puts the Rules of the Game out in the open, so you, your agent, and everyone else can see them and follow them.

You→Your agent→Rules of the GameEarth3→Everything you need

Because the rules are set up front, your agent can act for you without stopping to ask about every step.
